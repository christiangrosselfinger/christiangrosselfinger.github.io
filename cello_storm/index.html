The Show

Rock, metal and classical collide — and the room feels every bit of it.

Christian Grosselfinger moves between cello, electric guitar and keyboard without leaving the stage, tearing through AC/DC and Muse alongside Ravel’s Boléro, and driving through his own compositions — Metal Jack, Pushing Forward, 3 Little Girls. Some of it is built live with looping, layer by layer in front of the audience; most of it isn’t. What carries the show isn’t the technique — it’s the intensity.

There is a moment in every show where the audience stops watching an instrument and starts feeling a rock concert — the cello screams like a guitar, the room gets loud, and the faces in the front row change. That moment is the whole point.

The sound of a full act. The logistics of a solo artist.

For Venues & Promoters

Cello Storm looks big and travels small. Everything runs internally through the artist’s own system — if you have a PA and a mixer, there is almost nothing for you to prepare.

On stage
1 musician — optionally with drummer
Duration
60–90 min, straight through or with interval
Minimum stage
3 × 2 m solo — larger with drums
Set-up
1 hour with house PA & lights
Venue provides
2 × XLR (stereo) · power on stage · one chair without arms
Not needed
Monitors, DIs, mics — all carried by the artist

A 2-channel desk is sufficient. Christian can also bring his own PA and full lighting rig for venues without them, and is happy to share the bill with a local act.
